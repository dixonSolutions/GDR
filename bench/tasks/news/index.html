Vellmar Herald · Thursday edition

THE VELLMAR HERALD

Waterfront

Harbour terminal cleared for construction

The port authority has approved the long-delayed passenger terminal on the east quay, ending a planning dispute that ran for most of a decade. Work begins in the spring.

The completed terminal will operate nine berths, three more than the structure it replaces, and is expected to absorb the overflow that has forced smaller vessels to moor at the fishing pier since the storm damage of two winters ago.

Objections centred on the loss of the open quayside walk. The authority has promised a replacement promenade along the northern edge, though no date has been set for it.

Coastal defence

Seawall works to run through winter

Reinforcement of the western seawall will continue past the original completion date, with the contractor citing an unexpectedly soft clay layer beneath the foundation.

The project engineer, Halloran, told the council that the revised method involves driving piles a further four metres and that the additional cost falls within the contingency already agreed.

Residents of the western terraces have complained about night working. The contractor says piling is restricted to daylight hours and that the night activity is limited to concrete curing checks.

Science

Observatory marks a quiet anniversary

The hill observatory, founded in 1887, has passed another year with almost no public notice, which its director suggests is exactly how the institution prefers things.

Its original refractor is still in service, though the dome mechanism was replaced after a failure left the shutter frozen open through a week of rain. The instrument survived; the floor did not.

A public viewing evening is planned once the replacement flooring is laid, with dates to be announced through the usual channels.

Transport

Repairs to shut a commuter route

Track renewal will close the Harbour Line for eleven weeks from the end of the month, with a replacement bus service running at roughly half the usual frequency.

The operator has advised passengers travelling to the shipyards to use the northern route and change at the Salt Market, adding around twenty minutes to a typical journey.

Season ticket holders will receive a partial refund, applied automatically, though the operator has not said when the credit will appear.
